# CTA Section — Design Knowledge

> Source: Mobbin research across 20 SaaS product Landing Page CTA sections (2026-09-28)

---

## Pattern 1: Dark Full-Width CTA

**When to use**:
- Page bottom needs a strong visual anchor
- Primary goal is sign-up / trial conversion
- Page body uses a light background and needs contrast

**Execution**:
- Dark background (#0A–#1A range), occupying full width
- Centered alignment: large headline → [optional subheadline] → CTA button
- Headline tone can be more direct than the Hero ("Start building today" vs. Hero's product positioning description)
- CTA button uses light / brand color; ensure contrast against dark background
- Generous top and bottom padding to create a "pause" feeling

**Variants**:
- **With email input**: input field + button side by side, reducing sign-up steps (Kajabi, The Leap)
- **With dual CTA**: primary button + secondary link ("See pricing" + "Contact sales") — serves both self-serve and enterprise customers (Claude)
- **With social proof**: subtitle embeds data ("3,000+ businesses trust us")

**When not to use**:
- Page overall uses a dark theme (dark CTA would blend into the background)
- Immediately following a dark Hero (visually monotonous)

**Evidence**: Kajabi, Slash, Dollar Shave Club, OpenTable, Leonardo AI and 8 other products
**Evidence status**: Cross-sample inference

## Pattern 2: Minimal White CTA

**When to use**:
- Brand tone is restrained and professional
- Sufficient persuasive content has already been shown above
- Page overall leans light

**Execution**:
- White or near-white background
- Minimal content: headline + single button (no subheadline, no form)
- Button is the visual focal point — filled brand-color button
- Generous whitespace; add no decorative elements

**Evidence**: Notion, Patreon, Wix, Duolingo
**Evidence status**: Observed

---

## Pattern 3: Dual-Channel CTA

**When to use**:
- Product has two distinct target audiences (e.g., developers + decision-makers)
- Need to provide different conversion paths simultaneously

**Execution**:
- Two-column layout: left side one type of CTA, right side another
- Example: left "See pricing" + "Contact sales", right newsletter sign-up form (Claude)
- Example: left newsletter subscription, right product tutorial entry (MetaMask)
- The two columns don't have to be visually equal — the column with the primary goal is more prominent

**When not to use**:
- Single conversion goal
- On narrow screens, the two stacked columns don't form a clear information flow

**Evidence**: Claude, MetaMask, Uniswap
**Evidence status**: Observed; small sample

## CTA Copywriting Strategy

| Style | Example | When to use |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| **Action-oriented** | "Get Started", "Start for free", "Try it now" | Self-serve sign-up product; clear conversion path |
| **Consultation-oriented** | "Contact sales", "Book a demo", "Talk to us" | Enterprise product; sales involvement needed |
| **Personalized** | "Yep, Still $10" (Dollar Shave Club) | Strong brand personality; consumer-facing |
| **Low-pressure** | "Explore plans", "Learn more", "View docs" | User still in the consideration stage |

**Bottom CTA vs. Hero CTA**:
- Bottom CTA copy is typically more direct than the Hero — users who have scrolled to the bottom have shown some level of interest
- Hero: "Get Started" → Bottom: "Start your free trial today"
- Avoid identical copy — give the bottom CTA a sense of time or certainty

## Supporting Elements Alongside CTA

| Element | Effect | Example |
|---------|--------|---------|
| Reassurance text | Eliminates hesitation | "No credit card required", "Free forever" |
| One-line social proof | Adds trust | "Join 3,000+ businesses" |
| Privacy statement | Compliance + reduces perceived risk | "By proceeding you agree to our Terms" |
| Time commitment | Reduces perceived cost | "Set up in 5 minutes" |
