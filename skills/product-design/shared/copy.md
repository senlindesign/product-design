# Copy — Headlines, Section Headings, CTAs

> Read this before writing any headline, section heading, eyebrow label, or button label.
> Evidence: 72 real headlines from 56 products (Mobbin Sites, 2026-10-08, 10 queries across SaaS, developer, AI, fintech, design-tool, and consumer-app heroes, feature headings, CTA bands, pricing headers, and testimonial headings). Counts below are over those 72 unless stated. The sample favors well-designed sites, so it describes good practice, not the average web. Lines marked *Inference* are a reading across samples, not a proven rule.

**Scope.** This file decides *wording*. It does not change structure (page skill) or visuals (style file). It never permits a claim the user did not give: any speed, scale, result, or customer claim still follows the master's *Sample content* rule (`data-sample`, listed in the QA report).

---

## 1. Targets at a glance

| Measure | Target | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Hero headline length | **4–8 words**; aim for ~5 | Hero median 5.5 words (n = 32, range 2–12); 50 / 72 headlines are 6 words or fewer. The 9–12-word headlines were mostly newsletter blocks or three-sentence announcements. |
| Section heading length | **3–7 words** | Median 5 (n = 40); testimonial headings median 4. |
| Case | **Sentence case** | 58 / 72 (81%) sentence case; Title Case 3 / 72 (4%); no hero used a Title Case claim. ALL CAPS (9 / 72) is a display-type choice of specific brands (Wise ×4) — do not use it unless the brand does. |
| Concrete object | **Hero names a concrete thing** — the product category or the object the reader works with (revenue, CRM, API, banking, currencies, gift card, documentation, app) | 25 / 32 heroes (78%); 43 / 72 overall (60%). |
| Questions | **Do not write a headline as a question** | 1 / 72. Questions appear in subheads and eyebrows instead ("Ready to make the leap?", "The key to success?"). Exception: a page skill with its own evidence for question titles wins — the Contact page header "How can we help?" (`pages/contact-page/references/page-header.md`) and the Pricing header "Question type" (`pages/pricing-page/references/pricing-header.md`). This sample had no contact headers. |
| End punctuation | Optional; a period is fine, "!" almost never | 20 / 72 end with "." or "!"; the only "!" is "Get Started Now!" (Craft). |
| Subheading | Usually present, one sentence, ~15 words | 52 / 72 have one; median 15 words. |
| Buttons per block | 1 or 2 | 2 buttons: 34 · 1: 29 · 0: 8 · 3: 1. |

*Inference:* the strongest single signal of human-written copy is a concrete noun in the hero. Abstract heroes that worked used a strong voice instead (incident.io, Figma, Sketch) — a higher-skill move and a poor default.

## 2. Headline formulas

Ten formulas cover 65 of the 72 headlines (the other 7 are hype-verb headlines — §3 — plus one question and one proof claim). Examples are **verbatim**, case as displayed.

| Code | Formula | Use it for | Real examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| B | **Verb + concrete object** — imperative verb, then the thing the reader manages or gains | Heroes (most common hero formula, 6 / 32); pricing headers that state the pricing principle | "Build systems to grow revenue" (Clay) · "Manage all your currencies all over the world" (Wise) · "Pay only for what your app uses, by the second" (Railway) |
| A | **Category claim** — "The [qualifier] [category] (for [audience])" | Heroes when the category is known and the qualifier is true (5 / 32 heroes) | "The ultimate CRM for startups." (Attio) · "The AI-native documentation platform" (GitBook) · "An AI detector that actually works." (Pangram) |
| D | **Two-beat fragments** — two short, often parallel sentences | Heroes and closing CTAs; good for a product with two clear halves | "One API. Every modality." (SpaceXAI) · "Powerful banking. Simplified finances." (Mercury) · "Built for the future. Available today." (Linear) |
| E | **Contrarian / negation** — names what is wrong elsewhere ("not", "no", "over", "actually") | Heroes for challengers; CTA bands that remove a risk | "The era of one-size-fits-all banking is over" (Slash) · "Not your average gift card." (Fluz) · "NO HIDDEN FEES OR MONTHLY SUBSCRIPTION" (Wise) |
| G | **Range / fits every size** — "from X to Y", "to fit every Y" | Pricing headers, scale-up stories, feature headings | "From Inception to IPO" (Sequence) · "Seamless selling from one location to one hundred" (Square) · "Pricing to fit every conversation" (WhatsApp) |
| S | **Scope / everywhere** — one thing for all places, times, or cases | Multi-platform, multi-currency, sync, consumer apps | "MONEY FOR HERE, THERE AND EVERYWHERE" (Wise) · "learn anytime, anywhere" (Duolingo) · "Creation and coordination, together in one place" (Dropbox Paper) |
| C | **Action invitation** — the headline is the action | **CTA bands and sign-up blocks** (9 of 11 uses are outside the hero); developer pages | "Start today" (Tines) · "Start building with the Notion API" (Notion) · "Take it for a spin" (Copy.ai) |
| W | **Voice line** — a human, slightly playful sentence | Secondary heroes (app pages, about pages) and feature headings, when the brand has a voice | "So good, you'll break things on purpose" (incident.io) · "Just enough to get things done." (Aboard) · "Creative tools meet the internet." (Figma) |
| M | **Introduction** — "Meet…", "…starts here" | Launches and new products, especially AI agents | "Meet your 24/7 AI team" (Notion) · "Meet your first Bot" (SpaceXAI) |
| J | **Plain label** — names the section, no claim | **Testimonial, FAQ, and secondary feature headings** (5 of 7 uses) | "Customers" (Hex) · "What our customers say" (Mews) · "Building blocks for developers" (Notion) |

**Defaults by placement** (*inference* from the placement counts):
- Hero: B first; A when the category is the news; D or E for a sharper voice.
- Feature section heading: B, G, or W — a claim about the reader's work, not "Features".
- Social proof / FAQ heading: J. Plain is normal here.
- CTA band: C or E (risk removal). Never a question (§3).
- Pricing header: B or G that states the actual pricing principle ("Pay only for…", "Know exactly what you're paying for." — PayPal). *Inference:* the generic "Simple, transparent pricing" did not appear in the 7 pricing headers.

## 3. Phrase rules

### Banned outright (0 uses in the evidence)

| Phrase | Evidence |
|---|---|
| "Everything you need (to …)" | 0 in 72 headlines and ~1,700 words of supporting copy. Real sites name the thing instead: "all the tools you need" appears only in body copy (Sketch, Vizcom). |
| "Revolutionize" / "revolutionary" | 0 |
| "Ready to [verb] your [noun]?" as a CTA headline | 1 / 72, and that one named the brand ("Ready to see what Mailchimp can do for you?"). CTA bands use action invitations or risk removal instead. |
| "Unlock" as a metaphor ("Unlock the power of…") | 0 metaphorical uses; the one "unlock" was literal (a bike lock — VanMoof). |
| Title Case Claims Like This | 3 / 72 headlines use Title Case, and no hero states a claim in Title Case. Use sentence case. |

### Allowed with limits — "AI words"

Words such as **supercharge, seamless, streamline, empower, elevate, effortless, cutting-edge, next-generation, unleash, powerful, all-in-one** do appear on real sites. Of the typical "AI copy" words checked, supercharge, seamless, streamline, empower, unlock, elevate, effortless, cutting-edge, next-gen, and unleash total 18 occurrences, 8 of them in headlines. "Powerful" (8, 2 in headlines) and "all-in-one / one place" (8, 1 in a headline) are more common, mostly in body copy.

**Rule:** at most **one** such word per headline, and only when the same headline names a concrete object.

| Allowed (real) | Why |
|---|---|
| "Supercharge your marketing efforts" (Dub) | one hype word + named object |
| "Seamless selling from one location to one hundred" (Square) | one hype word + concrete range |
| "Make code reviews effortless" (Linear, card title) | one hype word + named object |

| Not allowed | Why |
|---|---|
| "The #1 AI Agent. The next generation Helpdesk. One seamless service suite." pattern | 3 hype terms in one hero (Intercom is the only such case in the sample — it is the outlier, not the model) |
| "Supercharge your workflow" | "workflow" is not a concrete object; say which work |
| "Unleash your potential" | no object at all |

*Inference:* the AI tell is **density and vagueness**, not single words. No hero in the sample combined a hype verb with a vague object.

### Sub-headings

- One sentence that says what the product does and for whom, in plain words. It carries the detail the headline left out.
- It may hold a number only if the user gave it ("Save up to 2x when you send, convert and withdraw 50 currencies" — Wise, a real figure from Wise).
- Questions are fine here ("Ready to make the leap?" — The Leap), but not more than one per page.

## 5. Section headings and eyebrow labels

**The problem:** generated pages put a small uppercase label above every heading ("FEATURES" → "Everything you need…"). Real pages rarely do.

**Evidence:** 12 of 72 headlines (17%) had an eyebrow label. Only 2 of those 12 were a generic section name ("FEATURES" — Aboard; "Testimonials" — Kalstore). The others named an **audience or product area**: "CRM FOR STARTUPS" (Attio), "For developers" (SpaceXAI), "POS pricing" (PayPal), "BRANDING TOOLS" (Shopify), "Rive Editor" (Rive). Feature headings with an eyebrow: 2 of 9.

**Rules:**
1. **At most 2 eyebrow labels per page** (*inference* from 17%: on a 7-section page that is about 1). The hero counts.
2. An eyebrow must add information the heading does not: the audience, the product area, or the chapter number ("1.0 Intake" — Linear). Never the section's type name ("Features", "Testimonials", "FAQ", "Pricing").
3. Sections without an eyebrow start directly with the heading. Do not replace the eyebrow with an icon or a badge.
4. Testimonial and FAQ headings may be plain labels ("Customers", "What our customers say") — 5 of 7 plain-label headings were testimonial, FAQ, or feature headings.
5. Vary the heading position with the layout (`shared/signature-moves.md` §2): split sections put the heading in the text column; a split header (heading left, paragraph right) is a real alternative to a centered heading (Square timeline, Linear product pages). Do not center every heading.

## 6. Buttons (CTA labels)

**Evidence (100 labels):** first word — Get 23 · Start 13 · Download 8 · Sign 6 · Book 6 · Contact 6 · Open 5 · Try 4 · Read 3 · See 3 · Request 2 · Learn 2. "Get started" (any form) 15; labels with "free" 26; with "demo" 11; with "trial" 6.

**Rules:**
1. Start with a verb. Default verbs, in order of observed use: **Get, Start, Download, Sign up, Book, Contact, Open, Try**.
2. Prefer a **product-specific verb + object** when the product has one: "Open an account" (Wise, Mercury, Slash), "Get API Key", "Read Docs", "Deploy a new project" (Railway), "Compare savings" (Wise), "Send money now".
3. Two buttons: primary = the main action ("Start free trial", "Get started"); secondary = the lower-commitment or sales action ("Book a demo", "Contact sales", "See our pricing").
4. Use sentence case ("Get started", not "Get Started"), unless the style or brand sets buttons in caps.
5. Risk-reducer microcopy under the button is common and useful **when true**: "No credit card required" (×3), "14-day free trial · No credit card required · Cancel anytime". These are product claims — use them only if the user stated the terms; otherwise tag them `data-sample`.
6. "Learn more" is the weakest label (2 / 100); use it only for text links inside cards, never as a primary button.
7. The CTA band headline and its button should say the same action ("Start building with the Notion API" → "Get started").

## 7. Self-check before delivery

For every headline and section heading on the page:

- [ ] Sentence case (unless the brand uses ALL CAPS display type).
- [ ] Hero is 4–8 words and names a concrete object or category.
- [ ] No headline is a question (except the page-skill exceptions in §1).
- [ ] None of the banned phrases in §3.
- [ ] At most one "AI word" per headline, attached to a concrete object.
- [ ] At most 2 eyebrow labels on the page; none is a bare section name.
- [ ] Every button starts with a verb; risk-reducer microcopy is either from the user or tagged `data-sample`.
- [ ] No two section headings use the same formula back to back (*inference* — the same idea as layout rule R2 in `shared/signature-moves.md`).
